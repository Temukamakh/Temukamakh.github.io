Wave Dash: omni-wheel football robot

A remote-controlled, four-wheel omnidirectional robot built by a team of two for the university robot football championship. I was responsible for the mechanical and electrical side.

What it is

The chassis is an X-shaped base plate with an omni wheel in each arm. Because every wheel rolls freely sideways, the four motor speeds can be combined to move the robot forward, sideways or diagonally, or to spin it on the spot. A deck above the motors carries the Arduino Mega and four BTS7960 motor drivers, and a shell with the robot's name cut into it closes the sides.

From mecanum to omni wheels

The first design used mecanum wheels, mounted like ordinary wheels. A shipping problem meant they would not arrive in time, so we switched to omni wheels borrowed from a friend. Four omni wheels only work when they are set at 45°, which meant redesigning the chassis completely.

Getting under 4 kg

The redesigned robot came out over its 4 kg mass budget. We ran topology studies in SolidWorks on the materials we had, then laser-cut away the material the studies showed was not needed. The finished robot weighs 3.9 kg.

Drive and control

Each wheel has its own planetary-geared DC motor. The robot is remote-controlled from my drone transmitter. The drive code is deliberately simple: it turns the stick inputs into the four wheel speeds, so the omni drive responds in a way that is logical to steer. A servo kicks the ball with precision.

Result

Everything worked as planned, and the final robot came in at 3.9 kg, just under the budget.
